### V. The Rules for PIC Proceedings

PIC has adopted rules governing its proceedings. $ ^{FN6} $ The Code of Conduct also sets forth rules governing how PIC is to proceed. $ ^{FN7} $ The process generally starts with the filing of a sworn complaint with PIC by a person alleging a violation of the Code of Conduct. $ ^{FN8} $ PIC then meets to review the complaint to determine if it is frivolous or states a violation. $ ^{FN9} $ If PIC determines that the complaint sets forth a violation, then PIC sets the matter down for a hearing. $ ^{FN10} $ PIC's legal counsel is the prosecutor at the hearing. $ ^{FN11} $ The complaint must be served on the person charged with violating the Code of Conduct. $ ^{FN12} $ The complaint must specifically identify each portion of the Code of Conduct that the person is alleged to have violated and the facts upon which each alleged violation is based. $ ^{FN13} $ The burden of proving violations of the Code of Conduct is on the prosecutor and such violations must be proven by clear and convincing evidence. $ ^{FN14} $ The clear and convincing evidentiary standard is an intermediate evidentiary standard, higher than mere preponderance, but lower than proof beyond a reasonable doubt. $ ^{FN15} $ The hearing is to proceed as follows:
