## Current assessments of U.S. military campaign, prospects for success

### Few say current military effort in Iraq and Syria is going well
- **Dec 2015**: 58% say the U.S. military campaign against Islamic militants in Iraq and Syria is going not too well (39%) or not at all well (19%); 35% say it is going very (7%) or fairly (28%) well.
- **July 2015**: 62% say the campaign is going not too well or not at all well; 30% say it is going very or fairly well.
- **Feb 2015**: 58% say the campaign is going not too well or not at all well; 36% say it is going very or fairly well.
- **Oct 2014**: 58% say the campaign is going not too well or not at all well; 34% say it is going very or fairly well.

### More long-term optimism in ISIS fight
- **July 2015**: 36% say U.S. and its allies will definitely/probably fail; 55% say they will definitely/probably succeed.
- **December 2015**: 27% say U.S. and its allies will definitely/probably fail; 66% say they will definitely/probably succeed.

### Partisan divides
- **Current assessments**: 45% of Democrats, 33% of independents, 26% of Republicans say the campaign is going at least fairly well.
- **Predictions of ultimate success**: 65% of Republicans, 72% of Democrats, 62% of independents say it will ultimately be successful.

## Turn 2 — document page 17 (rank 2 of 20)

## Views of the U.S. campaign against ISIS

- Overall, a 64% majority continues to say they approve of the U.S. military campaign against Islamic militants in Iraq and Syria, while just 28% say they disapprove.
- Support for the U.S. military effort has been steady over the course of 2015.
- Democrats (67%) and Republicans (69%) continue to be about equally likely to approve of the military campaign.
- Slightly fewer (60%) independents express approval.

## Turn 3 — document page 19 (rank 3 of 20)

## Partisans differ over greater concern about engagement, ground troops

- Overall, 50% say their greater concern is that the U.S. will not go far enough in stopping militants; 42% say their greater concern is that the U.S. will go too far getting involved.
- Republicans: 75% say greater concern is U.S. won’t go far enough; 18% say greater concern is U.S. will become too involved.
- Democrats: 61% say greater concern is U.S. will go too far; 33% say greater concern is U.S. won’t go far enough.
- Conservative Republicans: 81% say greater concern is U.S. won’t go far enough; 12% say greater concern is U.S. will become too involved.
- Liberal Democrats: 67% say greater concern is U.S. will become too involved; 27% say greater concern is U.S. won’t go far enough.
- Age differences: Adults under 30 are more concerned about going too far (55%) than not going far enough (37%).

## Public split over use of ground troops
- **Dec 2015**: 47% in favor, 47% opposed of sending U.S. ground troops to fight Islamic militants in Iraq and Syria.
- **July 2015**: 44% in favor, 49% opposed.
- **Feb 2015**: 47% in favor, 49% opposed.
- **Oct 2014**: 39% in favor, 55% opposed.

## Two-thirds of Republicans favor sending U.S. ground troops to Iraq and Syria
- **Dec 2015**: 66% of Republicans favor, 28% oppose.
- **Democrats**: 33% favor, 64% oppose.
- **Independents**: 48% favor, 47% oppose.
