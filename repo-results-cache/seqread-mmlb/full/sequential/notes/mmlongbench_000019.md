## Gestalt Principles of Visual Perception

### Closure.
We tend to perceive objects such as shapes, letters, pictures, etc., as being whole when they are not complete. Specifically, when parts of a whole picture are missing, our perception fills in the visual gap.

Below the text, there is an illustration showing two incomplete shapes:
- A dashed circle, with parts of the circumference missing, implying the perception of a complete circle.
- A dashed rectangle, with parts of the sides missing, implying the perception of a complete rectangle.

### Continuity.
Elements of objects tend to be grouped together, and therefore integrated into perceptual wholes if they are aligned within an object. In cases where there is an intersection between objects, individuals tend to perceive the two objects as two single uninterrupted entities.

Below the text, there is an illustration showing several vertical lines intersecting with a diagonal line, demonstrating how the visual system perceives the lines as continuous rather than broken.
