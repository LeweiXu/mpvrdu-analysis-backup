Skeptical attitudes about the benefits of technology: Older adults who do not currently use
the internet are divided on the question of whether that lack of access hurts them or not. Half of
these non-users (49%) agree with the statement that "people lacking internet access are at a real
disadvantage because of all the information they might be missing," with 25% agreeing strongly.
But 35% of these older non-internet users disagree that they are missing out on important
information—and 18% of them strongly disagree.
Difficulties learning to use new technologies: A significant majority of older adults say they
need assistance when it comes to using new digital devices. Just 18% would feel comfortable
learning to use a new technology device such as a smartphone or tablet on their own, while 77%
indicate they would need someone to help walk them through the process. And among seniors who
go online but do not currently use social networking sites such as Facebook or Twitter, 56% would
need assistance if they wanted to use these sites to connect with friends or family members.
