In our prior decision, we remanded for the district court to apply the correct legal test to the First and Fourteenth Amendment claim and to separately address the equal protection claim. But it shifted course and ruled for the Party on its First and Fourteenth Amendment claim.
To remedy that constitutional violation, the district court permanently enjoined the Secretary from enforcing the 5% signature requirement that applied to third-party and independent candidates for non-statewide office. In its place, the district court imposed a 1% requirement as an interim measure, which would persist until the state legislature enacted a permanent replacement. The Secretary and the Libertarian Party both appealed.
II.
We review a district court's decision on cross-motions for summary judgment de novo. Chavez v. Mercantil
\( ^{2} \)  The Libertarian Party moved for summary judgment on its classification theory underlying its equal protection claim, not its discriminatory purpose theory. The district court later found the discriminatory purpose theory moot in light of its conclusion on the First and Fourteenth Amendment claim. That theory is not at issue here.
