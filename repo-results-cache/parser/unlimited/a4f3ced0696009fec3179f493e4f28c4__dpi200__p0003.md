Each signer must declare that she is a registered voter of the electoral district qualified to vote in the next election for that office, sign her name, and include her residential address; signers are also encouraged to add their dates of birth for verification purposes. Id. § 21-2-170(c). Upon filing, the petition circulator must attach a notarized affidavit stating that, among other things, the signers were qualified to sign the petition, and then an official must verify the signatures. Id. §§ 21-2-170(d), 21-2-171(a). If a nomination petition is denied, that decision can be reviewed by a court through an application for a writ of mandamus. Id. § 21-2-171(c).
In addition to the petition requirement, prospective candidates for non-statewide office must file a notice of candidacy and submit a qualifying fee. Id. § 21-2-132(d). For most offices, including U.S. Representative, the fee is 3% of the office's annual salary. Id. § 21-2-131(a)(2). A candidate who cannot afford the fee may file a pauper's affidavit instead, which requires an affirmation under oath of an inability to pay, a financial statement, and a signed petition. Id. § 21-2-132(g)-(h).
