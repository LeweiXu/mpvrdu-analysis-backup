First, as Figure D shows, upzoned lots tended to be located in census tracts with a higher proportion of non-white residents than the median tract in the City. Downzoned lots, on the other hand, were more likely to be located in tracts with a higher share of non-Hispanic white residents than the City median, and contextual-only rezoned lots were located in areas with still higher shares of non-Hispanic white residents.
\( ^{4} \)  In 2004, the City adopted changes to the Zoning Resolution designating much of Staten Island a “Lower Density Growth Management Area.” This action added or enhanced several requirements for new residential development in affected areas, such as minimum off-street parking and rear yard dimensions. This resulted in an effective net decrease in capacity in Staten Island that is not reflected in our results, because the changes did not move lots into different zoning districts or explicitly alter their maximum FAR.
