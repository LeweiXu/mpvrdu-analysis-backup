Pertinent Terms Integral to Piaget’s Theory of Cognitive Development

Four pertinent theoretical concepts, which are integral to Piaget's theory of cognitive development and relate to intellectual structures, are: schemata; assimilation; accommodation; and equilibrium. Schemata are the mental structures by which individuals intellectually adapt to and organize their environment. They dictate a child's pattern of reasoning. Schemata of children may not coincide with that of adults, but each child's organization will always be internally consistent.

Assimilation is the cognitive process by which the individual integrates new stimuli into existing schemata. When a child assimilates he or she “…remains unaware of, or disregards, whatever does not fit into the conceptual structure it possesses” (Glasersfeld, 1995, p. 63).
