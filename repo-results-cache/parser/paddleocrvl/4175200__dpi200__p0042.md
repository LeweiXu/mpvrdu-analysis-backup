The impressive trade power of the European Union (EU) has enormous impact all over the world. And with it, many “externalities,” hidden costs not paid by buyers or sellers but transferred elsewhere.

In classic economic theory, trade is mutually beneficial almost by definition, because noncoerced economic actors will only buy or sell if they also gain something. In modern times international trade is analysed more closely. There are enormous asymmetries in power and information among participants in global markets, which results in some participants benefitting more than others and, as a result, trade increases inequalities instead of acting as “communicating vases” where the liquid reaches the same level regardless of the shape or volume of the containers.
