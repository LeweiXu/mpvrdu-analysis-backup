There is a great dilemma in Western spiritual consciousness: we have lost faith in the traditional ways we experience our place in the universe. Dostoyevsky in The Possessed says it best: "God is necessary, and so must exist... Yet I know that he doesn't exist, and can't exist... But don't you understand that a man with two such ideas cannot go on living?" In Spiritual Atheism, Steve Antinoff argues that atheism begins with three realizations: our experience of ourselves and the world is dissatisfying; our dissatisfaction is intolerable and must be broken; and there is no God. Continuing in the vein of Christopher Hitchens and Sam Harris, Antinoff makes a critical contribution to the understanding of salvation and enlightenment.

“Ronald Aronson demonstrates that atheism represents much more than what one does not believe: that it is the precondition for a generous humanism. The two closing chapters are models of stoicism at its best.”

—Christopher Hitchens, author of God Is Not Great

Ronald Aronson's Living Without God explores the issues of twenty-first-century secularists. Reflecting on such perplexing questions as to why we are grateful for life's gifts, who or what is responsible for inequalities, and how to live in the face of aging and dying, Living Without God is less interested in attacking religion than in developing a positive philosophy for atheists, agnostics, secular humanists, skeptics, and freethinkers.
