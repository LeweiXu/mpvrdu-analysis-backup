### 3.2 Freedom of movement

Movement rights for beneficiaries of complementary protection are often limited. Half of the systems do not allow freedom of movement within the country’s territory. In Germany $ ^{62} $ and Switzerland, $ ^{63} $ protected people must remain in an assigned district or canton. In Denmark, $ ^{64} $ they must remain for three years, or risk being deprived of social security benefits. Sweden $ ^{65} $ also has restrictions, but they are the same as apply to every alien in Swedish territory. $ ^{66} $

Eight systems impose conditions for access to international travel documents. Normally, they are only granted to those unable to acquire one in their countries of origin. $ ^{67} $ The German, the two Austrian and one Swiss mechanism $ ^{68} $ do not allow any possibility of getting a travel document. This same restriction also applies to subsidiary protection in Germany and Austria.

### 3.3 Family reunification

The right to family reunification is also limited. It is guaranteed only in Sweden and by two other mechanisms, one in Finland $ ^{69} $ and one in Switzerland. $ ^{70} $ One of the Austrian instruments concedes it, but only for family members overseas. $ ^{71} $ Denmark imposes restrictions like age limits, $ ^{72} $ while Swiss and British mechanisms impose waiting periods. $ ^{73} $ In the latter, the right to family reunification is only guaranteed after the person acquires indefinite leave to remain, which happens after six or ten years.
