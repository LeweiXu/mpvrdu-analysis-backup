Notice that we have shown that the Seely isomorphisms  $ s_{0}\colon S(1)\to\top $ and  $ s_{2}\colon S(A\times B)\to S(A)\otimes S(B) $ are present. These isomorphisms, which are so central to the structure of linear logic, play an important role in what follows.

The observation above means that a storage category always has (persistent and strong) representation of the tensor unit. Thus, we now focus on understanding monoidal representation. A key observation is:

3.2.5. LEMMA. In a Cartesian storage category X,  $ f\colon A\times B\to C $ is bilinear if and only if its classifying linear map  $ f^{\sharp}\colon S(A\times B)\to C $ coequalizes the linear maps  $ (S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon))s_{2}^{-1} $ and  $ (\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)s_{2}^{-1} $.

We shall use tensor notation even though we are not assuming that we have monoidal representation: this is justified by the fact that, for these particular objects, we are always guaranteed representation by Lemma 3.2.4. The translation of these maps,  $  S(\epsilon) \otimes S(\epsilon)  $ and  $  \epsilon_{S(A)} \otimes \epsilon_{S(A)}  $, back into tensor-free notation uses the commuting diagrams below:

 $$ \begin{array}{c}S(S(A)\times S(B))\xrightarrow{S(\epsilon\times\epsilon)}S(A\times B)\\ \left.\begin{array}{c}\\ s_{2}\\\downarrow\\\end{array}\right.\\ S^{2}(A)\otimes S^{2}(B)\xrightarrow{S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon)}S(A)\otimes S(B)\end{array} $$ 

 $$ \begin{array}{c}S(S(A)\times S(B))\xrightarrow{\quad\epsilon\quad}S(A)\times S(B)\xrightarrow{\quad m_{\times}\quad}S(A\times B)\\S^{2}(A)\otimes S^{2}(B)\xrightarrow{\quad\varphi_{\otimes}\quad}S(A)\otimes S(B)\end{array} $$ 

PROOF. We shall use the characterization of bilinear maps in Proposition 2.3.7 to show that coequalizing these maps is equivalent to bilinearity.

Assume that  $ (S(\epsilon) \otimes S(\epsilon))s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp} = (\epsilon \otimes \epsilon)s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp} $ so that

 $$ (\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}(S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon))s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp}=(\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp} $$ 

But then we have:

 $$ \begin{aligned}&(\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}(S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon))s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=(\varphi\times\varphi)(S(\epsilon)\times S(\epsilon))\varphi_{\otimes}s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=(\epsilon\times\epsilon)(\varphi\times\varphi)m_{\times}f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=(\epsilon\times\epsilon)\varphi f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=(\epsilon\times\epsilon)f\\&(\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=(\varphi\times\varphi)(\epsilon\times\epsilon)\varphi_{\otimes}s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=\quad\varphi_{\otimes}s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp}\\&\quad=\quad m_{\times}S(f)\epsilon\end{aligned} $$
