## Jerker Fick

Assistans Professor, Department of Chemistry, Umeå University. jerker.fick@chem.umu.se

Jerker Fick is a pharmacist and a researcher within environmental chemistry. He performs research about the fate of pharmaceuticals in the environment and potential bioaccumulation in aquatic organisms. In addition to that, he is investigating different kinds of environmental effects such as the development of antibiotic immunity.

## Ethel Forsberg

Director General, the Swedish Chemicals Agency

Ethel Forsberg has been the Director General of the Swedish Chemicals Agency (KemI) since 2001. She has a Master of Science Degree in Landscaping Architecture. Before joining KemI she held positions at several sectors within the Swedish society: as environmental manager in the Federation of Swedish Farmers, senior administrative Officer at the Ministry of the Environment, Manager of Food Safety, Environment and Consumer Affairs at COOP (Head office of Cooperative Retailers in Sweden) and as Director of Administration of Health, Food and Environment in the City of Stockholm. At present she is on the board of European Chemicals Agency (ECHA).

## Bo Gunnarsson

Doctor of Medical Science, Executive Office Support Apoteket AB.

bo.gunnarsson@apoteket.se

Bo Gunnarsson is also a pharmacist and has since 1971 been in different positions on the Swedish drug market. He has published about 50 scientific papers regarding medical, pharmaceutical and environmental subjects. In 1999 he becomes the Head of Environmental Department in Apoteket (National Corporation of Swedish Pharmacies) with focus on drugs in the environment.

## Lina Gunnarsson

Postdoctoral Fellow, the Sahlgrenska Academy at the University of Gothenburg. lina.gunnarsson@fysiologi.gu.se

Lina Gunnarsson is a molecular biologist by training. She received her PhD in 2009 from the University of Gothenburg on a thesis entitled 'On the use of genomics to assess environmental risks of pharmaceuticals'. Her most important contribution so far to the field is a study published in 2008 on 'Evolutionary conservation of human drug targets in organisms used for environmental risk assessments'.
