## A U STR A L I A

It's an old New World. When a country has had a winemaking history that dates back a century or more, how can that still be described as the New World? Good question. Well, it's a question of grapes – vitis vinifera grapes, to be exact, of which there are thousands of varieties, native to the Mediterranean, and grown for centuries in countries such as France, Italy, Spain, Germany, Portugal, et al. The grapes were brought with settlers during (and after) the age of exploration, to New World countries such as the United States, Australia, South Africa, Chile, Argentina and New Zealand. Nowadays they regularly beat Old World equivalents in blind tastings, thanks to Steven Spurrier and the Judgement of Paris. You might notice that the majority of our New World wines are from Australia, the United States and South Africa. This is because we think some of the most exciting winemaking is happening there right now. Plus, our head sommelier, Gareth Ferreira, knows more than most about the latter, as he happens to hail from the South Africa. So we hope you will join us on our journey, whether to the Pacific Ocean-influenced hills of the Santa Maria Valley in California's Central Coast, or to the open skies and gum tree-lined roads of south Australia's Clare Valley, or to the maverick winemakers causing a stir in Swartland.
