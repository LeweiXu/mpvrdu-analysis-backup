#### 6.8.4 Underwriting and Operating Results

Overall, the general insurance industry recorded an underwriting surplus of MK732.5million during the year under review from MK267.2 million recorded in 2008 (Chart 9). Nevertheless, the industry combined ratio (claims ratio and expense ratio) of 101.1 percent was worse than the 93.6 percent, 94.6 percent and 98.0 percent reported in 2008, 2007 and 2006 respectively. This is not a healthy situation as it means that the industry is paying out more money as claims and other expenses, than it is receiving as premiums. On the other hand, the industry experienced a drop in investment income during 2009. Total investment income reduced to MK348.6 million compared to MK632.3 million for 2008. This decrease is mainly attributed to poor performance of equity investments on the stock market during the year.

As a result of the drop in investment income, the consolidated pre-tax profit for general insurers at the end of 2009 stood at MK761.0 million, down from MK863.9 million in 2008. Income tax expense decreased to MK206.0 million from MK218.7 million recorded in 2008 while the industry's post tax profit for the year declined to MK555.0 million, from MK645.2 million reported in 2008.
