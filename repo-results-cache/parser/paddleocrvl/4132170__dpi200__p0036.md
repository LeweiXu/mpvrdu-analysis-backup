When a Cartesian storage category has strong monoidal representation, then this representation is automatically persistent. To establish this we start by observing that a Cartesian storage category already has a fair amount of monoidal representation.

###### 3.2.4. LEMMA. In any Cartesian storage category

(i)  $ \varphi: 1 \rightarrow S(1) $ gives persistent unit monoidal representation;

(ii)  $  m_{\times}\colon S(A) \times S(B) \to S(A \times B)  $ gives persistent monoidal representation for the objects  $  S(A)  $ and  $  S(B)  $.

PROOF. We shall focus on the binary monoidal representation: first note that  $ m_{\times} $ is bilinear as  $ m_{\times} = \theta S(\theta^{\prime})\epsilon = \theta^{\prime}S(\theta)\epsilon $ where the last two maps of each expansion are linear while  $ \theta $ is linear in its second argument while  $ \theta^{\prime} $ is linear in its first.

The universal property for an arbitrary bilinear h is given by the following diagram, valid in any slice X[X]:

 $$ A\times B\xrightarrow{\varphi\times\varphi}S(A)\times S(B)\xrightarrow{h=m((\varphi\times\varphi)h)^{\sharp}}Z\quad\left.\begin{array}{c}\varphi\\ \downarrow_{m\times}\end{array}\right.\quad((\varphi\times\varphi)h)^{\sharp} $$ 

where h must be the solution to the simultaneous classification. But this makes the righthand triangle commute by uniqueness of the universal property.

Furthermore this is a persistent representation as the classification is persistent.
