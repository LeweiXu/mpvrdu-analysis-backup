#### 13.5.3 Case study: Passive Observer for Heading Autopilots Using both Compass and Angular Rate Sensor Measurements

It is advantageous to integrate gyro and compass measurements in the observer. This results in less variance and better accuracy of the state estimates. One simple way to do this is to treat the gyro measurements as an input to the system model

 $$ \dot{\psi}=u_{\mathrm{ars}}+b_{\mathrm{ars}} $$ 

where  $ b_{ars} $ denotes the gyro bias and  $ u_{ars} $ is the ARS measurement.

This model will give proper wave filtering of the state  $ \Psi $. However, the estimate of r is not wave filtered, since this signal is taken directly from the gyro measurement  $ u_{gyro} $. This can be solved by filtering  $ u_{gyro} $ with a notch filter  $ h_{\mathrm{notch}}(s) $ and a low-pass filter to the cost of some phase lag.

 $$ \begin{aligned}\dot{\hat{\xi}}_{w}&=\hat{\psi}_{w}+K_{1}\operatorname{ssa}(y-\hat{\psi}-\hat{\psi}_{w})\\\dot{\hat{\psi}}_{w}&=-\omega_{0}^{2}\hat{\xi}_{w}-2\lambda\omega_{0}\hat{\psi}_{w}+K_{2}\operatorname{ssa}(y-\hat{\psi}-\hat{\psi}_{w})\\\dot{\hat{\psi}}&=u_{f}+\hat{b}_{\mathrm{ars}}+K_{3}\operatorname{ssa}(y-\hat{\psi}-\hat{\psi}_{w})\\\dot{\hat{b}}_{\mathrm{ars}}&=-\frac{1}{T_{b}}\hat{b}_{\mathrm{ars}}+K_{4}\operatorname{ssa}(y-\hat{\psi}-\hat{\psi}_{w})\end{aligned} $$ 

 $$ T_{b}\gg0 $$ 

 $$ u_{f}=h_{\mathrm{notch}}(s)h_{\mathrm{lp}}(s)u_{\mathrm{ars}} $$ 

 $$ \boldsymbol{k}=\left[\begin{array}{c}-2(1-\lambda)\frac{\omega_{c}}{\omega_{0}}\\ 2\omega_{0}(1-\lambda)\\ \omega_{c}\\ K_{4}\\ K_{5}\end{array}\right] $$
