#### 2.1.5 Numa Cache

System z uses a Non-Uniform Memory Architecture (NUMA) approach to share a cache between all 77 processing units (PU) of a z10 system, and all 96 processors of a z196 system.

<div style="text-align: center;"><img src="imgs/img_in_image_box_380_423_1271_1265.jpg" alt="Image" width="53%" /></div>


A maximum z10 system can have a total of 77 processing units (PU) in 4 books. Of these, 64 may be configured as CPUs; the remainder are SAPs and spares. PUs are packaged into “books”, and a System z mainframe may have up to 4 books.

The four L2 caches of the four books are interconnected by point-to-point links and form a single common and shared NUMA L2 cache that is used by the 77 PUs in all four books  $ [25] $.

This is a unique System z feature. In other large systems, e.g from HP or Sun, the L2 cache is not shared, or is shared by a few CPUs at best.
