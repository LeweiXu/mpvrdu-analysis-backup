In these four Figures above, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5 and 6.6, we can clearly observe two main things: Firstly  $ SOC\_{F}(t)\cdot E_{batt} $ which is reaching full charge just 15 [%] of the time for  $ P\_LIM_{batt} = 5 $ [MW] which is only 12.5 [%] approximately, here we can see that in terms of battery full capacity charged we need to increase  $ P\_LIM_{batt} $ values otherwise most of the time we’ll be below full charge situation.

Secondly, how  $  P_{curtailed}(t)  $ is behaving when I'm increasing  $ P_{-LIM_{batt}} $ from 5 [MW] till 75 [MW], also it can be seen that there is no change detected from 50 to 75 [MW], having the same  $  P_{curtailed}(t)  $ value. In this case I would say that there is no justified reason for increasing  $ P_{-LIM_{batt}} $ parameter value, but we have to take into account that the other main reason of including BESS is minimizing forecast error. So we'll need to check first about it and after to make the right decision looking at the whole picture.

Finally we can see how is  $ P_{\_}\mathit{HPP\_F}(t) $ just reaching  $ P_{grid} $ between the:  $ [25\% : 27.5\%] $ of the time depending on the  $ P_{\_}\mathit{LIM}_{batt} $ value taken. Here you can see another difference with the real time model where  $ P_{\_}\mathit{HPP}(t) $ can't manage to reach  $ P_{grid} $ during as much time as bidding model does.
