Similarly, for a strong monoidal unit representation to be persistent requires, setting  $ A = A_{1} \times B \times A_{2} $ in (iv), that whenever p is linear in B then  $ p^{\top[A]} $ is also linear in B.

As before, the basic form of monoidal representation assumes only that it holds in the original category: to be a strong monoidal representation requires the property must also hold in every simple slice. Thus, as for classification, there is a progression of notions: monoidal representation, strong monoidal representation, and persistent strong monoidal representation, each of which demands more than the last. This also applies to the unit representation; however, as we shall shortly discover (see Lemma 3.2.4), every storage category already has a persistent strong unit representation. Thus, we shall often talk of monoidal representation when we mean both monoidal and unit representation.

We first observe that persistence produces a simultaneous universal property:

3.2.2. LEMMA. If  $ f\colon A\times X\times Y\times Z\to W $ is linear in its last two arguments, then there are unique maps linear in their last arguments,  $ f_{1}\colon A\times X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)\to W $ and  $ f_{2}\colon A\times(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\to W $ such that  $ (1\times((1\times\varphi)\varphi))f_{1}=f=(1\times((\varphi\times1)\varphi))f_{2} $.

This is useful in the proof of:

3.2.3. PROPOSITION. If X has a system of linear maps for which there is a persistent strong monoidal (and unit) representation, then  $ \otimes $ is a symmetric tensor product with unit  $ \top $ on the subcategory of linear maps, L[].
