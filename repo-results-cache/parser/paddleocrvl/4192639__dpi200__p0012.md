# REPORTS FROM THE ZEN WARS

# The Impossible Rigor of a Questioning Life

STEVE ANTINOFF

Memoir • Trade Paperback • 9781619027312. $16.95 • 288 pages • 6" x 9" • Ebook available

Four decades ago, Steve Antinoff experienced a “negative satori”: the irrefutable realization of himself as a predicament only enlightenment could resolve. This brought him to Japan, where he lived and studied Zen for fifteen years. There, he encountered various occupants of the Zen world: Toyoshima-san, who astounded and inspired while impaled on a cliff's edge; Thief, whose yawns and teeth brushing shot sparks of Absolute Meaning; Hisamatsu, who overheard a doctor say he'd be dead in six months, only to awaken ten years later. These are among several portraits in Reports from the Zen Wars, Antinoff's attempt to bear witness to what, for him, has been the price of admission of the lotus position.

# THE OLD TEA SELLER Life and Zen Poetry in 18th-Century Kyoto

BAISAÖ

TRANSLATED BY NORMAN WADDELL

Poetry / History • Trade Paperback • 9781582434827 $14.95 • 240 pages • 5" x 8" • Ebook available

Baisaō was an influential and unconventional figure in a culturally rich time in Kyoto. A Buddhist priest, he left temple life behind at the age of forty-nine and traveled to Kyoto, where he began to make his living by selling tea. Yet Baisaō dispensed so much more: though he would never purport to be a Zen master, many influential artists and thinkers considered his shop a religious gathering place.

The Old Tea Seller includes virtually all of Baisaö’s writings, translated for the first time into English, along with the first biography of Baisaö to appear in any language. It establishes Baisaö’s place alongside other Zen-inspired poets such as Bashö and Ryokan.
