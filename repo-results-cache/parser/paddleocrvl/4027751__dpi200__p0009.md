From this point onwards, industrial robots took massive leaps in the correct direction. For instance, in 1974 when Victor Scheinman developed the Silver Arm. It was paired up with sensors to improve accuracy. On top of the joint sensors that detect the movement of the joints, the silver arm was integrated with touch and pressure sensors acquiring from the robot constant feedback and increasing its precision.

Some of the robots became compact enabling work on small objects. But there was one feature that all the newer robots possessed: an increase in precision. When referring to the precision of a robotic arm, it is the ability of an industrial robot to bring its end effector to the same position and orientation, repeatedly. However, no robot is perfectly accurate, because there is always a margin for error. A main factor affecting precision in an industrial robot is the gearboxes in its joints (encoders).
