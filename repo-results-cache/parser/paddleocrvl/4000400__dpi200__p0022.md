Of 3,000 employees at the Sheffield Steel plant in Houston, approximately 1,300 were Negroes, all of whom belonged to the integrated Steelworkers Local 2708. That union bargained with the company through a Joint Seniority Committee composed of two Negroes and two whites. In 1956, over the opposition of its Negro delegates, the committee signed an agreement for the gradual elimination of the plant's dual seniority system. Thenceforward, (1) No. 1 line jobs would be open to all bidders, subject to the company's screening, (2) the 260-hour probation period for No. 1 jobs would be abolished and replaced by industrial tests, (3) Negroes who passed the test would be given preference for No. 1 vacancies, and (4) if no Negroes (from the No. 2 line) could bid on No. 1 jobs, such jobs would be open to bidders from an integrated labor pool who had passed the tests.
