Also note the existence of  $ \backslash imath $ and  $ \backslash jmath $, which produce dotless versions of “i” and “j”. (See Table 145 on page 46.) These are useful when the accent is supposed to replace the dot. For example, “ $ \backslash hat\{\backslash imath\} $” produces a correct “i”, while “ $ \backslash hat\{i\} $” would yield the rather odd-looking “i”.
