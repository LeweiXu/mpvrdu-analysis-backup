## Contribution of schemes to producer welfare

All four crops for which there are stabilization schemes are almost exclusively exported. The only exception is coconuts, where a small proportion of production is consumed domestically. In this situation, the effects of the schemes on the welfare of domestic consumers can be ignored. Also, because Papua New Guinea contributes such a small share (no more than three per cent) of world exports of these products, it is a price-taker in the international market. Producers face a set of almost perfectly elastic demand curves. These two facts make it relatively simple to analyse the welfare effects of the country's agricultural stabilization schemes.
