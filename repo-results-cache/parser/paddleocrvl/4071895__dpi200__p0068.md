#### 2.5. EXERCISES

Exercise 2.20. For two formal power series  $ a = a(x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}, \ldots) $ and  $ b = b(x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}, \ldots) $ show that with notational convention (2.14),

 $$ b=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{1}{k}a_{k}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}b_{k}. $$ 

Hint: Use the following classic property of the Möbius function  $ \mu $:

 $$ \sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=\begin{cases}1,&if n=1,\\ 0,&otherwise.\end{cases} $$ 

Exercise 2.21. a) Taking the logarithm of the equality  $ (2.19) $, b) and using Exercise 2.20, prove the explicit formula  $ (2.20) $, namely

 $$ Z_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\cdots)=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{\phi(k)}{k}\log\frac{1}{1-x_{k}}. $$ 

Hint: Use the following formula for the Euler  $ \phi $-function:  $ \phi(n) = \sum_{d|n} d \mu(n/d) $.

b) Deduce from a) the remarkable relation

 $$ \frac{x}{1-x}=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{\phi(k)}{k}\log\frac{1}{1-x^{k}}. $$
