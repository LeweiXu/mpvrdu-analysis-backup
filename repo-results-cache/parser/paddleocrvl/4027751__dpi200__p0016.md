Using the formula:  $  T = rF\sin(\theta)  $, torque was calculated at each joint.

r is the radius in cm from the pivot/joint in cm, F is the Force/weight of the objects in Newtons(N),  $ \Theta $ is the angle between the force and the radius in degree, and T is the torque in Kg.cm.

Torque calculations for the Shoulder:

The torque calculation of the shoulder servo is done by taking into consideration all the weights that succeeds it as well as their corresponding distances in cm from the servo itself.

taking g = 9.81 and  $ \theta = 90^{\circ} $

 $$ \begin{aligned}&T_{_{net}}=(0.032\cdot0.06\cdot g)+(0.073\cdot0.12\cdot g)+(0.1086\cdot0.179\cdot g)\\ &+（0.0134\cdot0.238\cdot g）+（0.0524\cdot0.301\cdot g）=0.48N\cdot m=4.8kg\cdot cm\\ \end{aligned} $$ 

From the above calculations, we conclude that the shoulder servo needs to provide a minimum torque of 4.8 kg·cm to lift the parts that succeed it in case of the harshest configuration (when the arm is fully extended) and when the arm is not carrying any load.

A similar approach is then applied to the rest of the servos.

This means that the shoulder needs a servo with a torque of 4.8 kg-cm to function during the harshest scenario. This equation was applied for the rest of the servos.
