## B U R G U N D Y

Talk to any Burgundy buff and it won’t be long before the word ‘terroir’ crops up. The term, bandied around the wine world, carries so much meaning and weight, and refers to all the natural elements – the geography, the geology and the climate, which together with the winemakers’ skills creates a unique vintage. But just when you’ve got your head around that, up pops the word ‘climat’, which is unique to the wines of Burgundy. Climat is a precisely delineated vine plot, with its own microclimate, and specific geological condition, shaped by human cultivation over centuries. The Gallo-Romans grew vines here. In the Middle Ages, monks carved up their vineyards into tiny patches, marking their boundaries with dry-stone walls. Along the Côte de Nuits and the Côte de Beaune, home of the greatest Burgundies, there are more than 1,200 such sites, clustered around villages, methods passed down from generation to generation – and this, in a nutshell, is what makes Burgundy so special.
