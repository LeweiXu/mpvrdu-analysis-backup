### ARTICLE 2. Subdivision of Land

The sale, development, and subdivision of land are vital functions of the Town of Little Elm as they affect the community's quality of life. The requirements of the Subdivision Ordinance are intended to establish a fair and rational procedure for developing and subdividing land such that the land will be developed in accordance with existing physical, social, economic, and environmental conditions.

Article 2 of the Development Handbook focuses on the platting process and auxiliary plans required during the platting of land. A large development, such as a shopping center, office complex, or residential subdivision generally follows a two-step process. The Preliminary Plat indicates in general terms where lots and blocks will be placed, provides preliminary drainage and utility plans to ensure that the tract will function as proposed, and establishes entrances from existing and proposed roadways. Following approval of the Preliminary Plat, a Final Plat may be submitted for all of the area shown on the Preliminary Plat or only a portion of the Preliminary Plat, should the development be constructed in phases. Therefore, a housing community might have several phases of several dozen home lots along with “common area” lots (i.e. those lots that are provided for recreation or aesthetics and are owned and maintained by the Homeowners’ Association). Each of these phases would require a Final Plat.

A Preliminary Plat/Final Plat sequence is not required for a small development, such as a stand-alone restaurant on a tract of land that already has frontage on a road and the utilities in place – a Final Plat or in some cases, a Minor Plat may be used under these conditions (see the Subdivision Ordinance, Article 107.04 for more detailed information regarding types of plats and how they are used).

Other plats include an Amending Plat, which corrects mistakes that may have been made on the original filed plat. These mistakes are of a minor nature and they do not add or remove any lots. An Amending Plat may be approved administratively. A Replat, which may divide one lot into two or more lots or relocate a lot boundary is the one type of plat in which a public hearing is necessary (see Section 107.03.05 of the Subdivision Ordinance for more information regarding the plat notification process). A Conveyance Plat is used to sell property to a new owner who has no immediate plans to develop. No development may occur following the filing of a Conveyance Plat. However, one additional function of a Conveyance Plat is to dedicate access easements, fire lanes, or other easements that may assist an adjacent land owner in the development of his/her property.
