## B A R O L O

Is Barolo the new Burgundy? The land of wine, that's what the Ancient Greeks used to call Italy. And for good reason – there are vines everywhere, from the foothills of the Alps to the tiny Italian island of Pantelleria off the Sicilian coast. But it wasn't until the Second World War that Italy really started to catch up. Now Italy's top wines rival the best from Bordeaux, with serious reds – and even whites – coming out of pretty much every region. Though to really understand Italy's wines, it's best to think of it as a group of regions, rather than a single country. Its greatest reds? Barolo, say many – and it's to where many a Burgundy nut now heads when they run out, lured by Nebbiolo's structure and tannins. Like Burgundy, multiple producers may own small parcels within famous vineyards here; but unlike Burgundy, the best Barolo vineyards are not ranked as premier or grand cru, though this is always under discussion. It's no surprise then that vineyard prices are creeping up.
