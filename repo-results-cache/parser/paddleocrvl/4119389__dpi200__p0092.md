## Low sex drive (libido)

Some men feel less interested in sex after treatment.

There are different reasons for this:

• Coping with cancer and its treatment can cause anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem.

• Tiredness (fatigue) may carry on for months after treatment.

• Late bladder or bowel effects may lower your sex drive because they affect the way you feel about yourself sexually.

• Occasionally, men who have had pelvic radiotherapy make less testosterone. This hormone is important for sex drive.

You may find it helpful to get emotional support. Some people find it helpful to talk to a counsellor or sex therapist (see pages 121 to 122). As the late effects improve, you may start to feel better about yourself sexually.

If blood tests show you have low testosterone levels, your specialist may prescribe testosterone replacement therapy for you. They can tell you whether this is likely to be helpful for you. If you have had prostate cancer, it may not be suitable.

## Changes to the anus and rectum

After radiotherapy, you may need to be cautious with anal sex. The tissues in the area may be fragile and less able to heal. It may make anal sex uncomfortable or impossible. Your doctor can tell you more about this.
