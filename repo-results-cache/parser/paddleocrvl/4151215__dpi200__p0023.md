The earnings gap varied greatly by theatre. The worst offenders included Irish Repertory Theatre, The New Group, Second Stage Theater, Primary Stages, WP Theater and Lincoln Center Theater, which all spent less than $0.60 on BIPOC actors for every $1.00 spent on White actors. Yet despite the majority of theaters spending more on White actors, 7 of the non-profits actually spent more on BIPOC actors —Playwrights Horizons, Vineyard Theatre, Signature Theatre, Atlantic Theater, MCC Theater, New York Theatre Workshop, and the Public Theater.

Note: it is also highly likely that a significant earnings gap exists on Broadway where it is general practice for principal actors to negotiate higher-than-minimum contractual salaries but not as common for chorus members to do so. However, as negotiated salaries are not published, it is impossible to discern earnings disparity for Broadway at this time.



For every $1.00 spent on White actors, the non-profits spent $0.71 on BIPOC actors during the 2018-19 season. Broken down by race, $0.53 was spent on Black actors, $0.10 on Asian Americans, $0.05 on Latinx, $0.02 on MENA, and $0.00 on Indigenous actors.

## SPOTLIGHT: Lincoln Center Theater: Earnings Gap and Redlining

The Lincoln Center Theater had the third highest earnings gap of all the non-profits this year. This gap would no doubt have been even worse had the study included their production of MY FAIR LADY which opened in the 2017-18 season but continued to run this season at their Broadway house, the Vivian Beaumont Theater (only shows that opened during the 2018-19 season are included as part of this report). Based on last season's numbers, that show cast about two-thirds of their cast with White actors and had only White actors in leading roles. Salaries at the Beaumont pay almost 71% more than their smallest stage, the Claire Tow, a difference of $604/week and about 54% more than their mid-tier theater, the Mitzi Newhouse, or a difference of $463 per week. Of the 6 new shows LCT produced this season, only 1 was written by a BIPOC writer, MARYS SEACOLE by Jackie Sibblies Drury, which had an all-Black cast that was redlined to their smallest stage. (It is also worth noting that all three plays written by women playwrights were relegated to the smallest venue). LCT did present a play at the larger Newhouse which also had an all-Black cast, THE ROLLING STONE by White British playwright Chris Urch. The 10 Black actors in this show, however, were eclipsed by the number of White actors hired for 2 other shows at the Newhouse. Overall hiring on this tier was 62% White. Interestingly, the two White shows both ran an extra week longer than THE ROLLING STONE and two more weeks than MARYS SEACOLE. One of them, THE HARD PROBLEM by another White British playwright, Tom Stoppard, did cast 4 Asians...to play math geniuses...and perhaps only because the script stipulated race.



Overall, LCT only cast 38% of all roles with BIPOC actors and had an earnings disparity of $0.59 spent on BIPOC actors for every $1.00 spent on White actors. This broke down to about $0.39 for Black actors, $0.05 for Latinx actors, $0.15 for Asian American actors, and $0.00 for MENA and Indigenous actors.
