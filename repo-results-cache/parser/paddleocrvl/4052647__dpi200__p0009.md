I thought back to the work I’d done for the previous two years as a Peace Corps volunteer. This work ultimately shaped my work in RYF. During the Peace Corps, I was equipped with a vague task – to work with youth, and increase civic participation by youth in my small community. Obviously, I did not immediately achieve this goal. I didn’t speak the language, and more importantly, there was not trust between local youth and me.

It took about a year to really feel a sense of trust emerging between community members and me. Once that existed, work options suddenly opened. I felt most rewarded by the projects that my youth group and I led – refurbishing an out-of-use library, teaching younger kids about recycling, promoting gender equality in our small community. I remember sensing that many local adults viewed our work with skepticism because we lived, youth weren't typically viewed as being capable of civic activism. Adults doubted what 16 and 17 year olds could do, and this doubt seeped into teenagers as well. Some struggled to believe in themselves, but any time we successfully carried out a project they realized their own capacity to give back to their community. I would later observe similar discord between adults and RYF students, and a lack of mutual trust between those groups. I eventually used this experience when mentoring RYF students – when they were frustrated, distrustful, or jaded by adults, I helped remind them of their own self-efficacy and pointed to adults who did believe in them and who would support them.
