A number of substations and switchyards stations were put into operation in Serbia and other countries: SS Lome in Togo in 1963, SS Pnom Phen in Cambodia in 1967, SS Motato in Guinea in 1970, SS El Mullak in Egypt in 1970. Based on Energoprojekt designs, thousands of miles of transmission lines were built and commissioned: HPP Kpime-Palime, Togo in 1963; HPP Grandes Chutes-Conakry, Guinea in 1965; HPP Kirirom-Pnom Phen, Cambodia in 1967 and electrification of Sevala and Maskala cities, Mali in 1964.

Energoprojekt designed numerous public, business and residential buildings at home, such as the Panorama Hotel on Zlatar, Hotel Park in Budva, the Đerdap Hotel in Kladovo, motels in Vranje, Ovčar banja, Vlasina and many more. Energoprojekt realized architectural and construction projects in Guinea, Nigeria, Kuwait, Zambia, Uganda, and Central African Republic. In 1970, Energoprojekt designed and built a conference hall and 62 villas in the total area of 230.000 m $ ^{2} $ in Lusaka for only 107 days what was a true construction achievement also done in Uganda several years later on construction of a Conference hall in Kampala.

Energoprojekt continued to earmark a part of the income for professional education of the employees as well as for technical support of the work process. Consequently, the company purchased its first electronic computer "Elliott 803B" in 1963. In early 1964, "Elliott 803B" was put in operation and used for "solving scientific and technical problems", as explained at the time. Information technology later became one of the primary business activities of Energoprojekt.

After having the first design studies of nuclear technology and radioactive protection done, Energoprojekt did site selection for nuclear power plants in Yugoslavia(1963 to 1968). Videm Krško in Slovenia was one of these selected sites where the first nuclear power plant in Yugoslavia was put into operation at the beginning of the 1980s. Nuclear technology implied implementing high and strict quality and environmental protection standards which influenced positively on introducing modern design methods in other business activities of Energoprojekt.

Energoprojekt designed and built a factory for agricultural machines 'Zmaj' in Belgrade in 1963; abattoirs (slaughterhouses) and cold-storage houses in Mali (1964), in Ghana (1966), in Liberia (1969); spinning and weaving plants and leather factories in Tunisia and Algeria (1965-1967); fruit processing factory in Grocka (1966); goldsmith and flotation Bor (1970); steel mill in Macedonia (1967); oil refinery in Pančevo (1968).

Energoprojekt experts also designed water supply systems, sewage systems, irrigation and hydro-melioration systems in Serbia, Macedonia, Morocco, Burma, Jordan, Algeria, Syria, and Cyprus. Irrigation system projects were realized in Tikveš, Macedonia (1968); Polemidhia with a dam in Cyprus (1969); the Tafilalet region in Morocco (1970); Germasogeia with a dam in Cyprus; Kalimanci with a dam in Macedonia (1970); North Nawin in Burma (1971); Yarmouk in Jordan; Ibar-Lepenac in Serbia; Large Perimeters in Algeria and El Mullak in Egypt (1971).
