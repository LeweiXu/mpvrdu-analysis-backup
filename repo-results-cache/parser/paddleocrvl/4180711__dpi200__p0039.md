## B O R D E A U X

Ringing the changes in Bordeaux So you thought things hadn't changed much in France's most famous and largest wine region? Think again. There have been fundamental stylistic shifts in the region's wines over the last few decades. Thanks go in part to the simultaneous rise of the consultant and the wine critic (we're thinking of you, Michel Rolland and Robert Parker Jr). Their influence was – still is – massive, prompting an opulence in the wines not seen before. Who cares how these new super-ripe wines age? Not the public, certainly, who don't want to wait three decades to drink it. Yes, the region still lives and dies by the 1855 classification that ranks its top properties, but nearly everyone not at the top thinks this is now past its expiration date. And there's a growing number of earnest producers making laudable wines at artisan level, who are now farming with an eye on organics, and who revere the freshness and integrity that once made 'claret', as us Brits like to call it, so beloved.
