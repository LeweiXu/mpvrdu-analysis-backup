##### 6.9.4.2 Liabilities

Total liabilities for the sector increased by 6.6 percent to MK39.5 billion (2008: MK37.0 billion). The largest single liability as expected with the life insurance companies is the policy holder funds, which accounted for 94.3 percent of total liabilities. This is a slight decrease from the 97.9 percent recorded in 2008. Current liabilities increased markedly in the review period and at MK1.8 billion represented an increase of 132.4 percent to account for 4.7 percent of the total liabilities.

##### 6.9.4.3 Shareholder’s Funds

Total shareholder funds for the sector stood at MK12.1 billion (2008: MK13.7 billion). The decrease in capital was as a result of revaluation of shares which took a knock on the stock market. The revaluations resulted in a MK5.3 billion being recorded at the end of the review period as opposed to MK6.7 billion in 2008. Returned earning also registered a 8.3 percent decrease to close the period at MK5.0 billion, from MK5.5 billion.

### 6.10 INSURANCE BROKERS

#### 6.10.1 Performance Overview

Premiums handled by brokers during the year significantly increased by 19.2 percent to MK6.2 billion (2008: MK5.2 billion), representing 63.6 percent of total premiums for the insurance industry (Appendices Table 22). The amount of business handled by brokers is significantly higher in the general insurance, than the life insurance market, with 74.9 percent of premiums in general insurance passing through brokers, compared to 14.7 percent in the life insurance market in 2009. This clearly shows that most insurance brokers transact general insurance as opposed to life insurance business where most life insurers use tied agents who are individually recruited and trained by the companies.

Consolidated total assets for the broking industry amounted to MK2.8 billion in 2009 compared to MK1.8 billion the previous year. This means the industry registered an asset growth of almost 50.0 percent. In 2009, total brokerage commission $ ^{4} $ increased by 19.5 percent, from MK682.2 million in 2008 to MK815.4 million. The industry recorded consolidated profit for the year of MK172.5 million which compares favourably to MK93.4 million reported in 2008.

As at 31st December 2009, brokers had outstanding premiums due to insurers totalling MK1.9 billion (2008: MK1.4 billion). Out of this, MK1.2 billion (2008: MK593.5 million) was overdue for more than 60 days and this represents 62.5 percent of the total outstanding premiums. This increased level is attributed to the long credit period granted to brokers for remission of premiums.
