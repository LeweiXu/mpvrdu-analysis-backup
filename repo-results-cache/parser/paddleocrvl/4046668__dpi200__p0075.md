## Exchange rate policy

## The 'hard kina strategy'

From Independence until the early 1980s, the government pursued what became known as the 'hard kina strategy' as a key element in its macroeconomic policy. $ ^{3} $ The aim was to maintain relatively low inflation with free convertability of the kina, to maintain a strong balance of payments and to keep the level of foreign debt manageable without having the value of the kina depreciate against trading partners' currencies. This was perceived to be necessary to promote economic and political stability. In the absence of exchange controls and with wages indexed to the cost of living, this in effect required the government to use public expenditure and the inflow of foreign aid to maintain balance of payments equilibrium. The policy held firm through the 1970s, but when the terms of trade and balance of payments situation deteriorated during 1980-82 the government's approach changed.
