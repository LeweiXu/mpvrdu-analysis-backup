## NORTHERN RHÔNE

Did you know that there are vineyards along virtually the entire length of the Rhône River, from its source in the Swiss Alps to its mouth near Marseilles? The variety of wines is staggering, unsurprisingly. In wine terms, the valley is split into the northern and southern Rhône. The vineyards in the north, some of the oldest in France, are etched into the mountainside on narrow terraces at dizzying gradients, with the great majority of wine being made from the Syrah grape. The region's most famous wines are the deep-flavoured, long-lived reds of Hermitage and the generally more fragrant and delicate Côte-Rôtie. The southern Rhône, meanwhile, is more user-friendly (more boozy, too – we're looking at you, Châteauneuf-du-Pape). You can thank the openly fruity character of the Grenache grape, which rules here. And the change in scenery – it's the gateway to Provence, think olive trees, lavender and cicadas.
