I am pleased to present the 2009 annual report of the Supervision of Non-Bank Financial Institutions Department, which highlights the performance of the insurance and capital markets sectors and developments regarding microfinance and pension sectors in Malawi in the year ended 31st December 2009.

The non-bank financial sector encompasses insurance, securities, pension and microfinance. However, the Department’s current mandate is limited to insurance and capital markets due to the lack of enabling legislation and supervisory frameworks on pension and microfinance.

Overall the non bank financial sector in the country continued to experience growth in 2009 despite the continued difficulties experienced by the global financial industry due to the global crisis. The country was not greatly impacted due to its minimal integration with the global economy. However, comparatively the impact was more noticeable in 2009 than in the previous year as evidenced by lower levels of growth or stagnation in some sectors.

The general insurance industry recorded a 12.3 percent growth in total assets to MK9.5 billion while gross premiums were up by 25.2 percent at MK7.9 billion, from MK6.3 billion recorded in 2008. Gross claims escalated to MK3.0 billion during the year representing a 21.5 percent increase from MK2.5 billion reported in 2008.

Assets of the life insurance sector, at MK58.4 billion recorded a comparatively larger growth in the insurance industry, by 14.8 percent from MK50.9 billion recorded in 2008. However the industry gross premiums decreased by 9.9 percent to MK1.8 billion.

The stock market traded in negative territory during the period under review largely due to the effects of the global financial crisis. This was manifested through the exit of foreign funds. The market registered a decline in trade volume and value when it recorded 592.6 million shares at a consideration of MK2,877.6 million compared to 607.5 million shares at a value of MK8,390.1 million recorded in 2008. This represents a decrease of 2.5% and 65.7 percent in terms of volume and value respectively on account of falling prices following the second round effects of the global financial crisis. Consequently the Malawi All Share Index (MASI) closed the year down by 925.5 points – at 5154.9 points from 6080.4 points recorded in 2008.
