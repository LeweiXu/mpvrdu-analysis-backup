#### 4.3.10 Evaluation of standards for stability in digestate in other countries

## Organic acids (volatile fatty acids)

A review of English- and German-language sources was carried out by the researchers at the University of Southampton for WRAP (Banks et al., 2013) to establish the scientific rationale and basis for the adaptation of the VFA standard under the JRC end-of-waste criteria report. The evidence found that the measurement of VFAs is not adequate and this parameter is best used as an indicator of the stability process rather than of the quality of the final product used in soil. In addition, the German RAL quality standard for digestate, which includes a VFA limit, has a requirement for a minimum retention time in the digester to ensure effective degradation. Analysis of 20 samples was carried out and it was determined that the correlation between RBP and VFA is fairly good ( $ R^{2}=0.449; n=24 $) (Banks et al., 2013). There are very little grounds for using VFA concentration in Ireland as a product stability criterion. This parameter at its best indicates stability of the process rather than the product.
