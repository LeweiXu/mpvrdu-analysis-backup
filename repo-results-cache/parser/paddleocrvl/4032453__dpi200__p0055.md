Bee hive components and tools

Hive bodies. Hive bodies are the standard boxes that make up a beehive. Hive body, brood chamber, super, or bee box all mean the same thing – a housing structure for bees. The difference in name comes from how each is used.

A) Honey super box. Placed on top of the brood chamber where harvestable honey is produced. Comes in two sizes: shallow (5 3/8 inches) and medium (6 5/8 inches). A third, small size exists (4 3/4 inches) but is used only for the production of honeycomb. Honey supers are shorter than hive bodies because smaller boxes are lighter and easier to handle when full of honey. Super simply refers to their superior placement in the hive configuration – above the brood chamber.

B) Brood chamber. The tallest compartment in a hive is 9 5/8 inches tall and is reserved for housing the queen and “immature bees” – the brood; this box is also referred to as the deep super, brood nest, or hive chamber.
