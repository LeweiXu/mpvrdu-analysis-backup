So this condition implies bilinearity. Conversely, by reversing the argument, assuming bilinearity gives—using classification and monoidal representation—the equality of these maps.

We now have:

###### 3.2.6. PROPOSITION. X has (basic) monoidal representation at A and B if and only if

 $$ S^{2}(A)\otimes S^{2}(B)\xrightarrow[\epsilon\otimes\epsilon]{S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon)}S(A)\otimes S(B)\xrightarrow{\epsilon\otimes\epsilon}A\otimes B $$ 

is a coequalizer in the subcategory of linear maps.

PROOF. Suppose z equalizes  $  S(\epsilon) \otimes S(\epsilon)  $ and  $ \epsilon \otimes \epsilon $ then by the previous lemma  $  z' = (\varphi \times \varphi) \varphi_{\otimes} z  $ is bilinear and this determines a unique linear map  $ z'_{\otimes} $ with  $ z' = \varphi_{\otimes} z'_{\otimes} $.

 $$ \begin{array}{c}S^{2}(A)\times S^{2}(B)\xrightarrow[\varphi\otimes\downarrow]{S(\epsilon)\times S(\epsilon)}S(A)\times S(B)\xrightarrow[\varphi\otimes\downarrow]{\epsilon\times\epsilon}A\times B\\S^{2}(A)\otimes S^{2}(B)\xrightarrow[\epsilon\otimes\epsilon]{S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon)}S(A)\otimes S(B)\xrightarrow[\epsilon\otimes\epsilon]{\epsilon\times\epsilon}A\otimes B\\z\xrightarrow[\varphi\otimes\downarrow]{\varphi\otimes\downarrow}z^{\prime}\end{array} $$ 

We claim  $ z_{\otimes}^{\prime} $ is the unique comparison map making the fork a coequalizer in the linear map category. To show this we need  $ (\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)z_{\otimes}^{\prime}=z $ and we must show that any other linear map k with  $ (\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)k=z $ has  $ \varphi_{\otimes}k=z^{\prime} $. For the first of these we note that z is determined by representing  $ (\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}z=z^{\prime} $ but

 $$ (\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)z_{\otimes}^{\prime}=(\varphi\times\varphi)(\epsilon\times\epsilon)\varphi_{\otimes}z_{\otimes}^{\prime}=\varphi_{\otimes}z_{o}^{\prime}x=z^{\prime} $$ 

so the two maps are equal. For the second, suppose we have such a k then

 $$ \varphi_{\otimes}k=(\varphi\times\varphi)(\epsilon\times\epsilon)\varphi_{\otimes}k=(\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)k=(\varphi\times\varphi)\varphi_{\otimes}z=z^{\prime}. $$ 

For the converse assume that the linear map category has this fork a coequalizer and suppose  $ f\colon A\times B\to C $ is bilinear. Returning to the diagram above, by Lemma 3.2.5 we may set  $ z^{\prime}=f $ and  $ z=s_{2}^{-1}f^{\sharp} $. Noting that the top fork is an absolute coequalizer gives the map  $ \varphi_{\otimes}\colon A\times B\to A\otimes B $ which will then represent bilinear maps.

Considering what happens in an arbitrary simple slice gives:

3.2.7. COROLLARY. A storage category has strong monoidal representation if and only if forks of the form:

 $$ X\times S^{2}(A)\otimes S^{2}(B)\xrightarrow[1\times\epsilon\otimes\epsilon]{1\times S(\epsilon)\otimes S(\epsilon)}X\times S(A)\otimes S(B)\xrightarrow{1\times\epsilon\otimes\epsilon}X\times A\otimes B $$
