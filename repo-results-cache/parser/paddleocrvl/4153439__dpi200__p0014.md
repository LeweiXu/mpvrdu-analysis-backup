### B. Recreating Bankruptcy’s Debt Relief without the Bankruptcy Proceeding

Sadly, financial distress is not a recent phenomenon. $ ^{84} $ Despite the record number of recent bankruptcy filings, some statistics suggest that financial distress may have been as common in previous generations. According to Professor Charles Warren, approximately seventy-five thousand Americans were sent to debtor's prison each year in the years surrounding 1833. $ ^{85} $ If true, the ratio of the number of individuals sent to debtor's prison to the total population of the United States in 1833 is roughly equal to the ratio of the total number of bankruptcy filings in 2002 to the total population of the United States in that year. $ ^{86} $

While financial distress is not new, consumer bankruptcy is dramatically more common than in the past. The United States lacked a lasting bankruptcy system until the very end of the nineteenth century, $ ^{87} $ and bankruptcy remained relatively rare for generations thereafter. Annual non-business bankruptcy filings did not reach one hundred thousand until 1961; $ ^{88} $ by contrast, in 2003 there were over one-and-a-half million non-business bankruptcy filings. $ ^{89} $ These statistics understate the increase in bankruptcies because spouses could not file a joint petition until after 1978. $ ^{90} $
