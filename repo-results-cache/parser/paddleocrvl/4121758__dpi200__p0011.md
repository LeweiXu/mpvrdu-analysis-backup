Growth in the financial and insurance services slowed to 8.1 percent in 2009 compared to 11.7 percent in the preceding year. This resulted from slow performance in the insurance subsector due to rising operating costs, falsified claims and increasing incidences of unpaid premiums.

Monetary developments were expansionary in 2009 as the stock of money supply increased by 24.4 percent. The growth rate in money supply was lower than the 33.1 percent registered in the preceding year. The growth was on account of increased economic activity coupled with government's recourse to central bank resources. The counterparts to the monetary expansion in the year under review were firstly, an increase of MK27.8 billion in net credit to government and secondly, high economic activity which supported growth in the stock of outstanding loans and advances to the private sector which increased by MK26.9 billion. Monetary growth was however restrained by a decline of MK24.2 billion in net foreign assets. The decline was due to the fact that, in the absence of a successor arrangement for Malawi with the International Monetary Fund, the central bank continued to support the market, hence piling pressure on foreign reserves.

Annual headline inflation remained stable and lower compared to the previous year despite exogenous shocks emanating from the global financial crisis and fuel prices. The availability of food supplies however offset the impact of increased international costs, as average inflation eased to 8.4 percent in the review year from 8.7 percent in 2008. Looking ahead, inflationary forces are likely to begin to intensify in 2010 due to the depreciation of the Kwacha with respect to major currencies as this will have a pass-through effect to domestic inflation. In addition, pressures on prices are likely to build on account of global oil prices that have been oscillating above US$70 per barrel. On the other hand, the bank rate and liquidity reserves requirement remained unchanged throughout 2009 at 15.0 percent and 15.5 percent, respectively. Consequently, monetary policy was wholly implemented through open market operations.

The favourable economic environment prevalent in the review year positively benefited the financial system as its assets grew by 29.9 percent. The insurance industry assets increased by 14.5 percent as compared to the banking sector which grew by 25.1 percent. The expansion in the insurance sector mainly stemmed from the 14.8 percent growth in life insurance assets.

It is envisaged that with the positive economic outlook for 2010, where the economy is projected to grow by 6.5 percent, the performance of the financial sector will remain strong. In fact, the enactment of some financial sector laws such as the Insurance and Banking Acts in the year under review and the likely passing of the other laws in 2010 will improve the regulatory legal setting and hence induce growth in the financial sector. Nevertheless, low foreign exchange reserves, shifts in fuel prices and continued effects of the global crisis through reduced financing to local firms and low commodity prices for Malawi's exports are the focused challenges to the economy.
