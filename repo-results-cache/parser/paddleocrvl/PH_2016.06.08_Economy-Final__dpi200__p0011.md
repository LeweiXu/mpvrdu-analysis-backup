Between 2014 and 2015, Hispanic views of family income in relation to the cost of living were unchanged—about half of all Hispanic adults in both years said they were falling behind financially, as was true for the U.S. public as a whole.

By contrast, whites reported some improvement in their family income relative to the cost of living across the one-year time period. As a result, overall in 2015, the three racial and ethnic groups looked fairly similar on this measure.
