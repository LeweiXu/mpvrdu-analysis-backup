## Salmon Markets

### 6.7 Different sizes – different prices (Norway)

<div style="text-align: center;"><img src="imgs/img_in_chart_box_206_351_1439_879.jpg" alt="Image" width="74%" /></div>


The main reason for differences in size is the biological production process in which individual fish grow at different speeds. A farm holding fish of harvestable size will show a normally distributed size distribution. This leads to the majority of fish being harvested at 4/5 kg GWT with smaller quantities of smaller and larger fish.

The processing industry in Europe mainly uses 3-6 kg GWT but niche markets exist for smaller and larger fish. As these markets are minor compared to the main market, they are easily disrupted if quantities become too large. Generally, small fish are discounted, and large fish are sold at premium as shown in the graph above.

<div style="text-align: center;"><img src="imgs/img_in_chart_box_192_1376_1017_1838.jpg" alt="Image" width="49%" /></div>


The graph to the left shows Norwegian harvest distribution for 2020, with the harvest size of 4-5 kg (GWT) being the most frequent. In addition to catering for production process and market requirement, another driver behind this size

fluctuation is that farmers want to balance out market risk and biological risk. Drivers behind smaller harvest size can be disease, early harvest when there is a need for cash flow, or early harvest to realise ongoing capacity. Larger fish (6-7kg+) may be a result of economies of scale/lower production costs, production for niche markets or other market requirements.
