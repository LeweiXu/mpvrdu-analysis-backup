The load current decays exponentially (linearly if  $ R \rightarrow 0 $) and reaches zero when all the energy stored in the inductor is dissipated in the LSD and the load resistance.

External clamping circuitry (i.e. a freewheeling diode) could be used to protect the LSD in case the demagnetization energy exceeds the energy capability of the device. By using a standard freewheeling diode, the demagnetization voltage is reduced from  $ V_{clamp} - V_{batt} $ to approximately 1 V. This has an influence on the demagnetization time (lowering  $ |V_{DEMAG}| $  $ \Rightarrow $ increasing  $ T_{DEMAG} $).

## Calculation of dissipated energy

As previously mentioned, a typical characteristic of inductive loads is to maintain the direction and value of the actual current flow. Applying nominal voltage on inductive load (turn-on), it takes a certain time (depending on time constant  $ \tau = L/R $) to reach nominal current.

Removing the voltage source from the inductive load (turn-off), the load inductance tends to continue to drive the current via any available path (i.e. clamp of the LSD) by reversing its voltage (acts as a source) until the stored energy  $ (E_{L}=1/2\ L\ I_{O}^{2}) $ is dissipated.

The time needed to dissipate this energy is called demagnetization time ( $ T_{DEMAG} $). This time is strongly dependent on the voltage across the load ( $ V_{DEMAG} = V_{clamp} - V_{batt} $) at which the demagnetization is performed (higher  $ |V_{DEMAG}|\Rightarrow $ shorter  $ T_{DEMAG} $). A typical  $ V_{CLAMP} $ for M0-5 OMNIFET is 46 V.

The power dissipated ( $ P_{diss} $) by a device that drives an inductive load at given  $ V_{batt} $,  $ I_{load} $ and load characteristics is the following:

## Equation 18:

 $$  Pdiss=Pd,on+Pd,sw $$ 

in case of external freewheeling when the entire load energy is dissipated outside the OMNIFET.

It becomes:

## Equation 19:

 $$  Pdiss=Pd,on+Pd,demag $$ 

without freewheeling.
