The nontaxability of credits and other tax benefits can also be explained by the fact that they are effectively equivalent to a tax refund, and a tax refund is always excludible as a return of capital. $ ^{196} $ The exclusion for tax refunds (and other tax benefits) is in turn arguably based upon the nondeductibility of income taxes, because a recovery of capital is excludible only in case it was not deducted with tax benefit. $ ^{197} $ In the final analysis, however, Code section 275 is itself based upon the larger principle of avoiding circularity.

This same circularity principle should prevent Code sections 61(a)(12) and 111 from applying to OIC agreements and other statutory tax relief. Tax provisions which are designed to determine the amount of liability are structural rules of the tax system. These structural rules cannot themselves be treated as taxable events without interfering with their purpose. Taxing tax relief is just such a circularity, and permitting it would have the effect not only of complication and inconvenience, but of outright self-contradiction.
