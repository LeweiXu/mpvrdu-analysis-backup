#### 4.4.3 Job Satisfaction Gap is Not Attenuating

The assertion that the gender paradox is attenuating is examined in Table 8 (next page). First, Figure 8 shows that there is no evidence of attenuation during the 1997–2008 window, even with full controls. When Sousa-Poza and Sousa-Poza (2000) showed differences in job satisfaction by gender in a 1989 U.S. sample, Donohue and Heywood (2004) concluded that “the intervening nine years [between the survey that they have used and that used by Sousa-Poza and Sousa-Poza] are not sufficient for the absence of the gender effect among young workers to make itself apparent in their age-representative sample” (p. 213). In this sample, even the 28 year interval between the survey used by Donohue and Heywood and the 2008 wave of this survey were insufficient for the disappearance of the gender job satisfaction gap.
