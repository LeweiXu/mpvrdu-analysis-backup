I conducted the experiment three times under NM and once under FR-FM. In order to simplify data managing I developed a nomenclature for LD intervals relative to CT0 i.e. the moment illumination switched from white to blue light (Figure 5a). This way each interval was given a name consisting of abbreviation of light conditions utilized and a numerical suffix signifying the distance from CT0. Light interval abbreviations were as follows: WL- white light, BL- blue light and D- dark; while numerals were negative (-n) or positive (n) depending on weather the interval occurred prior to or following the light switch. I analyzed individual activity levels by distance traveled per hour (distance/h) of each LD interval (Figure 5b).

Both  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ and  $ L-cry^{+/+} $ animals showed a transitional activity pattern under NM, meaning their activity was increased at the time they anticipate darkness (BL1), but under FR-FM only  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ animals exhibited this behavior. Transitioning from WL-1 to BL1 animals showed no significant changes ( $ p \geq 0.05 $) in distance/h traveled, regardless of genotype ( $ L-cry^{-/-}/L-cry^{+/+} $) or lunar conditions (NM/FR-FM; Figure S2a-b). However, comparing BL1 to BL2 under NM (Figure 5c) both  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ and  $ L-cry^{+/+} $ were significantly more active during BL1 ( $ p < 1 \cdot 10^{-3} $), while under FR-FM only  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ showed an increase in BL1 activity (p = 0.042; Figure 5d).

To see if animals retain their nocturnal behavior in 16:8 BLD conditions I compared BL to D interval distance/h traveled. On average  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ and  $ L-cry^{+/+} $ animals were able to stay significantly nocturnal ( $ p < 1 \cdot 10^{-5} $; Figure 5e/g) under NM. Under FR-FM only  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ animals were nocturnal in the first ( $ p = 2.93 \times 10^{-3} $; Figure 5f) and the second ( $ p = 1.95 \times 10^{-3} $; Figure 5h) BLD cycle. This experiment showed  $ L-cry^{-/-} $ animals stay predominantly nocturnal in a periodic BLD illumination even if it is shifted from the LD cycle they are entrained to.
