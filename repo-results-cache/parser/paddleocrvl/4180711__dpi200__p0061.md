## SWEET WINE APPELLATIONS OF BORDEAUX

## Rot is a good thing

Some of the world’s best wine is sweet wine – fact. If you don’t believe us just see what happens when you whisper the words ‘Château d’Yquem’ into a wine lover’s ear. Some sweet wines are sweet just because they are made with ultra-ripe grapes (known as late harvest wines); others are made with something called noble rot, or botrytis cinerea, to give it its proper name. In the right kind of weather (moist, misty mornings, followed by warm, sunny afternoons), the airborne fungus dries out the ripe grapes, substantially increasing their sugar content and acidity, adding glycerol (that luscious, syrupy feel in the mouth), plus a host of new flavours. The downside is the grapes have to be picked by hand because they don’t all ripen this way together. The best botrytized wines include those from Sauternes in Bordeaux, Germany’s Trockenbeerenauslese and Beerenauslese, Austria’s Burgenland, Tokaji in Hungary, plus a line-up of special bottlings from all over the world.
