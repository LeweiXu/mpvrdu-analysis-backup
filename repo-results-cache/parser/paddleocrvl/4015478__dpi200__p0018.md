### 5.4 “Responsive” Industries

Throughout the remainder of the paper, we limit our sample to firms that are in “responsive” industries (i.e., industries for which TFP increased) so we can focus on what may be driving productivity gains. To identify which industries were “responsive,” we estimate the effects separately for every industry at the 2-digit CIC level.

It turns out that, even though there were no effects on TFP for heavier-polluting firms on average, productivity increases for three key “dirty” industries: smelting and pressing of ferrous metals (i.e., the steel industry), chemical materials and products, and textiles (see Appendix Table C.6). It is intuitive that the steel and chemicals industries responded, as they are extremely coal-intensive—they each account for 5-6% of total  $ SO_{2} $ emissions in China.
