### 1.1 Industrial Revolution

Since the dawn of time, humans and technological evolution were the results of a symbiotic effect. They provide balance by working together in harmony. This story dates back up to several million years ago, where the first indication of human traces appeared, and where technological advances were nonexistent. Fast forward to the present, humans and technology coexist in order to provide a more promising future. One of the main reasons for that is because technology helps us cut time and increase productivity and efficiency. The easy accessibility provided by modern technology allows the automation of manual tasks. This can change the whole dynamics of work, giving more time to more challenging ideas.

The first traces of factories began to appear around 1769 in Derbyshire, England. For the first time in history, the world would witness the employment of 300 people in a single area. Back then, the workers would sort and categorize all the objects by hand. The number of workers kept on rising until Edmund Cartwright designed the power loom in 1784. This machine marked the beginning of the industrial revolution ever since it was built in 1785, proving once again that technology increased profits and saved time. The number of power looms after that increased to an astronomical number of 250000.

The idea of a robotic arm was already founded thanks to sketches drawn by Leonardo Davinci that dated from 1480 to 1518 in which he envisioned a semi-autonomous robotic arm (figure 2). Nonetheless, the idea was finally realized in 1961 by the inventor Georges Devol, when it took the form of the 1814-kilogram robot nicknamed unimate (figure 3).
